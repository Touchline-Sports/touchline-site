The gap

The data arrives on Tuesday. The decision was needed on Monday.

Semi-professional clubs now own the same GPS units the professionals do. What they don't have is the sports scientist who turns the export into a decision, so it gets made on how the players look at warm-up instead.

Wellness

Thirty seconds, before they get out of bed.

Sleep, soreness, energy, mood. Two days before and two days after every match. Three weeks in, the system knows each player's normal, so a genuine dip shows up instead of hiding in the average.

Answers go straight to the coaching and medical staff. Compliance is the whole game here, which is why it fits on one screen.

Privacy

The physio's notes stay with the physio.

Every club asks this, usually second. Here is the answer before you have to.

Enforced in the database, not hidden in the interface. Players under 18 have nothing collected until a parent or guardian consents, and that consent can be withdrawn at any time. Data is stored in Australia.

For leagues and federations

One picture of the whole competition.

When clubs run Touchline, their league gets fixtures and ladders across every grade, player statistics by age group, a Team of the Week picked from the match sheet rather than a committee, and a fantasy competition that puts families in the league's news every week.

The club keeps its own data. The league sees counts, never a player's medical record.
